# Argument Summary — "Measuring the Struggle" (the GLP preprint)

Source: *Measuring the Struggle: Process Friction Traces as Independent Evidence of Genuine Learning in the Age of Generative AI*, Nik Bear Brown. Irreducibly Human Research Series preprint. This is the research paper the book expands into eleven chapters; this file is a one-page map of its argument.

## The argument in one line

The artifact used to be proof of the process; generative AI severed that link, so we must measure the process — the friction of genuine learning — directly, as a second evidence stream alongside the artifact.

## The move, in five steps

1. **The decoupling.** Assessment always rested on one causal assumption: only genuine learning could produce the artifact (essay, proof, project). The chain was Genuine Learning → Cognitive Process → Artifact. Generative AI inserts a bypass — AI Generation → Artifact — so the artifact no longer tells you which pathway produced it.

2. **Detection can't fix it.** Artifact-based AI detection is temporally bounded (trained on today's outputs, beaten by tomorrow's), asks the wrong question (did a human *type* this, not did a human *understand* this), and creates perverse incentives (students game the detector). The generation-vs-detection arms race has a structural winner, and it isn't detection.

3. **Measure learning, not the artifact.** Invoking the Bjorks' performance-vs-learning distinction: the artifact measures *performance* (temporary, observable), which AI maximizes while potentially zeroing out *learning* (durable, transferable). Learning leaves traces performance does not, because genuine learning is a biological event — dopamine prediction-error signaling, BDNF upregulation (~2.8×), dendritic spine formation (~37% under moderate load). An AI can produce the artifact without triggering any of these; it cannot produce the behavioral traces those events leave, because the events didn't happen. The fluency trap is the measurement hazard: smooth AI-processed understanding *feels* real and isn't, so a rough, searching artifact can be better evidence of engagement than a polished one.

4. **The GLP framework.** Genuine Learning Probability, GLP(S,C) = P(engagement genuine | Y), is a probabilistic, tier-calibrated score in [0,1] with a credible interval — a property of the *process*, not the artifact. It rests on **seven friction components**: Y₁ temporal engagement pattern (does time track difficulty or output length), Y₂ error trajectory coherence (do errors evolve along the concept's structure), Y₃ cross-context transfer (does understanding survive surface change), Y₄ uncertainty calibration (does confidence track correctness or inherit the AI's), Y₅ social knowledge texture (personal confusion vs. generic talking points), Y₆ retrieval strength decay signature (does the spacing effect appear), Y₇ scaffolding response curve (does a partial hint work — i.e., is there a real ZPD).

5. **Why the composite resists gaming.** A three-layer ensemble (per-component base models → tier-conditioned combination → meta-model) combines the seven. Each component is gamed by a *different* strategy, so faking all seven at once costs about what genuinely learning the material costs — at which point "gaming" has become learning in the only sense that matters. Two guarantees: the framework measures learning directly (it doesn't need to out-run generation tech), and the **instructor is the final meta-model** — GLP supplies a formally specified second stream; the weighting against artifact quality stays a professional judgment (a clinician weights process heavily; a writing instructor shipping an essay may weight the artifact heavily).

## The four claims, compressed

Genuine learning at each cognitive tier leaves characteristic friction traces (from the irreducible complexity of the neurology); those traces are partially independent of artifact quality; the composite score is far more gaming-robust than any single component; and GLP is a *second* evidence stream to combine with the artifact, not a replacement.

## What it explicitly does not claim

Artifacts aren't worthless (just insufficient as sole evidence); not every instructor must adopt the full framework; process isn't *always* more informative than the artifact (only that it adds *independent* information — the positive-ΔI information-gain test); and it never replaces instructor judgment. It also draws a hard line between process observation *for learning* (defensible) and *for surveillance* (not) — the same data, distinguished by use and control.

## Where it sits

Tier calibration inherits the Irreducibly Human seven-tier taxonomy. The paper is one instantiation of the broader **Frictional** framework (Frictional.xyz, in development): friction traces as cross-domain evidence of authentic human cognitive engagement — the same idea the author has applied to music-streaming contamination (the Musinique trilogy) and to AI-assisted coursework.
